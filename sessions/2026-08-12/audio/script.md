## ACT ONE — The water and the coin

VANDAL: *(measured, personal)* I was not with the four for this day's work; what follows is an account I have since gathered — from those who lived it, and from half the Dock Ward besides. Waterdeep talks, friend, if you buy the right rounds.

VANDAL: *(unfolding)* The bodies at the Deep Water Inn were not yet cold. The man behind the madness — the still point at the centre of all those white eyes — turned out to be no dark wizard at all. A dockhand. Grey, stubbled, ordinary as rope. Emmert Dorrow was his name, and when a crossbow bolt ended him, every white eye in that room cleared at once. His purse went to the barkeep, for his widow Thora. Whatever had worn him like a coat, it left him nothing but the seawater.

VANDAL: *(shifting)* Then the Watch arrived at Hal's shoulder — and here, friend, the tale grows awkward. For the guard asking questions was an elf. And the cursed blade out of the Nightstone crypt does not permit Hal Stormguard one civil word to an elf. The paladin ground his teeth, explained he carried a cursed sword — and was rude anyway. The guard bristled, told them all to stay put, and turned to fetch his captain.

VANDAL: *(sly)* Which is when small Fiz stepped in front of a room full of witnesses and asked the guard, sweet as you please, whether he recalled that his own eyes had gone white — that he himself had raised a blade against them. The man remembered nothing. So Fiz asked the room who did. Three patrons stood up. And the Watch, friend, discovered urgent guard business elsewhere.

[STING: bridge — 2s]

VANDAL: *(low, telling)* Then came the coin. The barkeep took it in his hand to look — and went blank. Gone, mid-sentence, eyes empty, until Fiz snatched it back and the man surfaced asking what he'd missed. Yet the coin did nothing to Fiz, who'd handled it all the while. Nothing to a patron who'd never been taken. Only those who had gone white-eyed, friend. The coin only touches the ones the sea has already touched.

VANDAL: *(quieter)* And Toz — Captain Tozlo Greenbottle, working the room with that easy halfling charm — learned one thing more. Every soul who'd been possessed had lately had the same dream. A dream of being called to the ocean. A piper, out of the sea.

VANDAL: *(darker)* The very dream, friend, that Toz himself has been having, night upon night. He told his crew none of it. And when the coin came round to his own hand, he made his face a wall. A good wall. But Eno Wazek has spent fifty years reading the stillness of wild things — and Eno caught the twitch. He alone knows his brother is hiding something. And he said nothing at all.

## ACT TWO — Grey tents at the Plinth

VANDAL: *(warm, storyteller)* A tip from a grateful patron sent them to the Plinth, in the Sea Ward — and this place I can vouch for myself, having busked its edges many a market-day. Rows upon rows of tents, one for every god who keeps no stone temple in the City of Splendours. Incense and chanting and, on a good day, a man floating upside down for reasons between himself and his god.

VANDAL: *(quieter)* At the very back stands a grey tent. Grey rug, grey robes, grey-haired woman kneeling in prayer, who said, without looking up — *someone approaches*. A priestess of Kelemvor, god of the dead. A gold coin went into her cup; Hal went onto his knees; grey smoke moved over her hands. And she gave the sword its true name.

VANDAL: *(quoted, cold)* "This is no ordinary curse. This is what we call grave goods. You stole this from a tomb. This does not belong to you."

VANDAL: *(measured)* Two remedies, friend, and only two. Carry the blade back to the sarcophagus beneath Nightstone where they found it — or find someone of its owner's lineage, and win their permission to bear it. And the sword belonged to the Nandars, the family of Nightstone, whose last known lady died under a giant's boulder. So for now the curse stands, and Hal carries his rudeness with him like a stone in the boot.

[STING: bridge — 2s]

VANDAL: *(wondering)* As for the eye — they searched every banner at the Plinth and found it on none of them. They found it on a wrist. A priestess wearing the coin's twin as a bracelet: eye on one face, corrosion creeping across the other. She served Umberlee. The Bitch Queen, sailors say, when they're safely ashore. The Queen of the Deeps.

VANDAL: *(telling)* An old symbol of the goddess, she said. Shine and rot at war on a single disc — life and death, each coin numbered on its corroded face, every number different. And when they told her of the tavern, she was quite clear: the coin is not cursed. What happened with it was merely — strange.

VANDAL: *(quoted, grave)* "The work of a warlock. Somebody who has tapped into the power of Umberlee, and is using it to their own means."

VANDAL: *(measured)* To trace such a caster, she said, seek a diviner. She blessed the whole crew in her goddess's name, whatever that proves worth. And to Toz, who confessed he sails under Valkur — she smiled, friend, and said Valkur will not always be there.

VANDAL: *(workmanlike)* Fiz, being Fiz, would not leave it at theology. He sat with the coin and cast Identify — ten patient minutes of ritual — and read it plain: a magic thing, no attunement, no charges, and exactly one spell upon it. Eternal Corrosion. Clean it, and the rot grows back. And in learning how the spell worked, the little artificer learned to work it himself — anything that fits in the palm of a gnome's hand, his to ruin forever. Locksmiths of Waterdeep, sleep uneasy.

## ACT THREE — The ledger and the company store

VANDAL: *(warm)* Down on the docks they found an old kindness waiting. Kuyrl Stonepalm — the goliath they had vouched for at the south gate, when a guard mistook giant-kin for giant — hauling two barrels at a time and greeting them warmly in the Giant tongue, for he speaks no Common at all. He is saving, he told them, for a pilgrimage. North, to the Spine of the World, to the great Oracle, to ask a question he would name to no one. And when a little booklet slipped from his torn pocket, they learned what he carries: his ledger of wages and debts — and folded within it, a map to the Oracle itself.

VANDAL: *(quoted, plain)* "The place of the Oracle is a place only for giants. I am giant-kin. I am a giant. I can open the way."

VANDAL: *(shifting, dry)* His debt was owed to a halfling called Brindle Stormtide, and Brindle they found holding court at the inn — perched on a box atop a chair, so he might look down on everyone, with a hired bard plucking flattery at his elbow. Brindle was all warmth to a fellow halfling, right up until the conversation turned to money. Asked about Kuyrl's debt, he laughed out loud.

VANDAL: *(quoted, cold)* "I'm gonna make a lot of money off that dude. That's all I'm gonna say. I think it'll be years."

VANDAL: *(dry)* Asked whether Kuyrl knew as much — maybe; he didn't know what the goliath knows. Asked after ship schedules, he took offence outright — am I your secretary now? — and large men began, unhurried, to drift closer. Even his translator, a lank fellow with a silver ponytail and an eye patch, when told in the Giant tongue that Kuyrl knew he was being swindled, answered only, flat as slate — what's he going to do about it?

[STING: bridge — 2s]

VANDAL: *(gathering)* So the crew went back to the docks and told the big man the truth, all four of them stacking their word behind it. He did not want to believe. He smashed a barrel to kindling not-believing it. And then Fiz asked for the ledger — let the money-man do the sums — and the sums, friend, were these.

VANDAL: *(dropping)* Eighty gold owed. And another eighty added every year he works. Because Brindle charges him for the very room he's kept in and the very food that keeps him hauling. The harder Kuyrl works, the deeper he sinks. The debt was built, from the first line of that ledger, never to be paid. There is a word for that arrangement, friend, and it is not employment.

VANDAL: *(steadier)* An overseer was already coming down the dock, drawn by the splinters. So the crew coached the goliath in a small theatre: bellow at us, they said, drive us off, and be seen to do it. He roared them off the boards most convincingly — and they left with his address, and a promise between them that not one copper of that debt would ever reach Brindle Stormtide's purse.

[STING: chime — 1s]

## ACT FOUR — Orders written in Draconic

VANDAL: *(measured, unfolding)* Evening took them across town to Chazlauth — the dragon-mad wizard, friend, wyrmling and all — for the loot off the half-dragon they'd slain at Goldenfields had never been properly read. The sigil ring came first. A badge, said Chazlauth, of the Cult of the Dragon: a faith in all but name, that worships Tiamat and labours for the ends of the chromatic wyrms.

VANDAL: *(low, telling)* Then he read the Draconic notes, and the room went quiet. Standing orders, from a master to the very half-dragon the crew killed. Take Zephyros's navigation orb. Avoid the storm giants. Observe the giant envoys — record, never interfere. Spread rumour and gold among the lesser giants, for — and here the orders quote themselves — *confusion is sufficient*.

VANDAL: *(quoted, cold, hushed)* "Ensure the old sites remain quiet. If signs of the Old Wyrm of the North appear, withdraw. Do not impede the designs of the deep speaker."

VANDAL: *(grave)* The Old Wyrm, Chazlauth reckoned, is Klauth — an ancient red dragon of the north, old as any wyrm alive, known to deal with small folk. Consider, friend, what it means that the Cult of the Dragon orders its own agents to run from him. But of the deep speaker, the wizard said nothing, for he knew nothing.

VANDAL: *(quieter, leaning)* I will not knit these threads for you. I only lay them side by side. The drowned of the Dock Ward all dream of a piper calling them into the sea. A warlock works Umberlee's power to some private end. And the Cult of the Dragon, on paper, in Draconic, defers to a *deep speaker*. Three threads, friend. One colour.

VANDAL: *(amused)* The visit ended in commerce, as visits to Chazlauth do. The blue-steel thieves' tools off the half-dragon — not magical, merely magnificent — he coveted purely for their looks, and traded for them a rod of dull metal that fixes itself in the air at the press of a button and will not be moved by all the tea in Calimshan. An immovable rod, into the crew's hands.

## ACT FIVE — Fog on the docks

VANDAL: *(quiet, drawing close)* Of what came after nightfall, friend, no song was sung in any tavern — the account here is sparer, but it is sure. They went by dark to Kuyrl's home: a one-room shack among the many crowded under the docks, where he keeps his wife and his son. The road, they told him, now ran through a Harper called Yackerty, and a portal, and Silverymoon beyond — the very road I set them on myself. The goliath agreed to work one day more, and wait for them the following night.

VANDAL: *(taut)* They were leaving the docks when the fog rose. Not rolled in, friend — rose, up out of the boards themselves, thick as wool, and swallowed the four of them whole.

VANDAL: *(urgent)* A blade came out of the murk at Eno's back — and missed. A spell cracked toward Fiz — and broke against his warding. And the little gnome spun on his heel and answered with Faerie Fire, flakes of burning metal fanning from his Arcane Firearm to paint five shapes in cold blue light. Then Eno called the wind, and the wind tore the fog to rags.

VANDAL: *(chilling)* And there in the blue glow stood the night's true face. A figure in grey robes, crusted stem to stern in barnacles, as if the harbour had grown him like a hull. Two dock workers, sword-armed and empty-eyed — puppets, like the tavern all over again. An octopus upon the boards, and another waiting in the black water below.

VANDAL: *(dropping)* Toz answered with a Thunderwave — and here the tale must be honest, because the crew were honest about it. The blast caught the barnacled thing, aye. It also caught one of the puppets. A dock worker, friend. Some ordinary man of the Dock Ward, no more the author of his sword-arm than the barkeep was of his blank stare — and the thunder killed him where he stood. The sea spent his life like a coin it had found in the street. That death sits on the docks tonight, and neither I nor the four will pretend otherwise.

VANDAL: *(quickening)* Then Hal was on the robed thing, and the cursed blade of the Nandars bit twice — and mark this, friend: the creature looked *astonished*. Astonished that any edge in this city could touch it at all. It broke for the water. Fiz's Scorching Ray took it at the dock's edge, all three beams burning home, and Hal's sword ended it as it leapt. It went into the harbour, and it sank.

VANDAL: *(low)* The surviving dock worker woke mid-stride, a stranger to his own drawn sword. The octopuses simply — lost interest, and slid away, the way the white eyes cleared when Emmert Dorrow died. Toz pushed them off with a wave. And the barnacled body, friend, was never recovered. The sea keeps its own. The sea, I begin to think, keeps a great many things.

## [CLOSING — 30s]

VANDAL: *(reflective, closing)* So the chapter closes with the harbour lying flat and innocent as a pressed sheet. Ahead of the four: a map to copy and a goliath's family to steal out from under a halfling's ledger. A diviner to find, who can put a name to Umberlee's warlock. A curse that waits on Nandar blood, and an old red wyrm in the north that even dragons fear. And beneath it all, a deep speaker whose designs go unimpeded — and a captain who dreams of a piper in the sea, and has told his crew nothing. Only Eno knows. And Eno is patient.

VANDAL: *(warm)* Rest well tonight, heroes. Tomorrow night, a shack under the docks, and a family waiting on your word.

VANDAL: *(warm, signature)* I am Vandal Lovelace. This has been a Tale of the True Hand.
