# Tales of the True Hand — 2026-06-02
## The Hunger of Queen Guh

---

## [COLD OPEN — 25s]

[MUSIC: low ember bed; an iron bell tolling, far off and urgent]

VANDAL: *(hushed, drawing close)* Listen. In the granary-town of Golden Fields, where the wheat stands tall around a smiling wooden goddess, the abbey bell had been ringing all night. First for goblins. Then for ogres. And now — as four weary heroes climbed the bell tower to look — for something worse. Hill giants, striding through the young wheat. Orcs at their heels, hauling catapults of green timber and knotted rope. And those catapults, friend, were not loaded with stones.

VANDAL: *(quoted, cold, hushed)* They were loaded with goblins.

## [TITLE — 8s]

[MUSIC: signature theme, brief]

VANDAL: *(bright, storyteller)* Well met, friend. Draw close to the fire. I am Vandal Lovelace, and this is a Tale of the True Hand. Tonight, the crew answers the bell at Golden Fields one last time — and learns, from a butcher's knife, the name of the hunger behind it all.

[MUSIC: settles under, becomes bed]

## ACT ONE — The bell

VANDAL: *(warm, unfolding)* Picture it, friend. Golden Fields — the great granary of the North, an abbey-town twice the size of little Nightstone, where a wooden statue of Chauntea stands smiling among the wheat and the harvest feeds half the Sword Coast. You will remember, from our last fire, how the goblins came in the night. Ogres and bugbears among them, sacks of pumpkins on their backs, and the Crew of the True Hand fighting them lane by lane until the raiders broke and ran. And you will remember how that tale ended — with the abbey bell tolling, braziers flaring on the watchtowers, and a guard's voice carrying on the wind. *The giants are coming.*

VANDAL: *(measured, aside)* Now — a word of honesty between us, for I was not on that wall. I had this account from the four themselves, a little while after, at the foot of a certain statue in the City of Splendours. But that meeting is its own tale, friend, and it keeps. Tonight belongs to Golden Fields.

VANDAL: *(quickening)* The bell would not stop. Iron on iron, over and over, and the streets filling with folk who did not know which way to run. So the crew told them. To the abbey — to the goddess's house — go, and bar the doors behind you. And at the abbey the abbot met them coming, and laid his hands on the two of the four worst-marked from the night's fighting, and their wounds closed like water smoothing over a thrown stone. A small kindness, friend. Mark it. In the great tales, small kindnesses become, in time, great things.

VANDAL: *(steadier)* And one did not run for the abbey. Zi Liang, an acolyte of Chauntea — armed, and steady, and known by name to the men on the wall. She led the four up into the bell tower, where the iron voice of the thing was so loud it near shook the teeth from their heads. And from that height, friend, they looked out over the wall and saw at last what the bell had been screaming all the night long.

[STING: chime — 1s]

## ACT TWO — Into the field

VANDAL: *(taut, unfolding)* Hill giants. Great slope-shouldered brutes, near as tall as the wall itself, coming on through the young wheat with clubs like felled trees. And around their ankles, a rabble of goblins and orcs — the small hungry company that gathers to a hill giant the way gulls gather to a fishing boat.

VANDAL: *(measured)* Zi Liang said the guards on the wall would need her more than the abbey did, and she went to them. And the four — being the four — did not stay behind the stone.

VANDAL: *(quickening, drawing close)* They went out. Over the wall and down into the open field, and then low into the standing wheat — stealing from haystack to haystack, quiet as poachers, closing the ground on those giants before ever a giant thought to turn its head. If they were to fight a thing the height of a house, friend, they would do it on their own terms, and at a time of their own choosing.

[STING: chime — 1s]

## ACT THREE — The two giants

VANDAL: *(measured)* Now, the fighting of it. I tell it as it was told to me — and the wall-guards of Golden Fields do not tell a thing small.

VANDAL: *(quickening)* Toz Greenbottle — captain of the drowned *True Hand*, halfling, with the storm living in his very blood — rose up out of the wheat and sent his lightning crackling into the giants' legs, and the air about him bit at everything near, for that is the way of a storm sorcerer roused. Fiz of Halruaa planted his Eldritch Cannon in the dirt of the field — that small thundering contraption of his — and it barked fire across the rows while his Arcane Firearm barked along with it. Hal Stormguard put himself under the first great club that fell, and when his blade bit back, it bit with the light of his oath behind it — a Divine Smite, friend, radiant as a debt repaid. And Eno — quiet Eno, Eldath's own — moved between them through the trampled stalks, closing wounds as fast as the giants could open them.

VANDAL: *(grave)* And all the while, friend, the sky was raining goblins. For the orcs had their catapults at work behind the wall — and they were flinging their own. Goblins in spiked helmets, hurled up and over and down into the town, each one bursting on the stones like a dropped melon. A cruel arithmetic. Every goblin a thief delivered inside the walls, and never a gate broken to do it.

VANDAL: *(measured)* But two giants was the sum of what came for them, and two giants the crew brought down — the first, and then the last — crashing into the very wheat they had come to steal. And when the giants fell, the little ones lost their nerve. The crew climbed the wall to look out over the far side, and watched the goblins and the orcs streaming away into the distance — the orcs dragging their crooked engines behind them through the ruined rows.

VANDAL: *(warm)* Golden Fields held, friend. Battered, scorched, short a few roofs and more than a few sheaves — but held. Twice raided since the crew came to it, and twice still standing.

[STING: bridge — 2s]

## ACT FOUR — The butcher's knife

VANDAL: *(softer, unfolding)* And now the turn of the tale. For when the crew came down among the giant dead, it was Fiz — sharp-eyed Fiz of Halruaa — who turned up, on one of the bodies, a thing that asked more questions than any sword ever could.

VANDAL: *(measured, telling)* A knife. A skinning knife — but sized for no hand that will ever shake yours, friend. A blade the length of your arm. A haft you could not close your fist around. An edge curved for one trade, and one trade only. Not a weapon. A *tool*. The tool of a butcher who dresses his meat where it falls.

VANDAL: *(drawing close)* And standing there among the corpses, with that knife in their hands, the four of them read the whole bloody season at last, the way an old sailor reads weather. The raids on the granary-town. The bugbears slipping over walls with sacks of vegetables. The emptied livestock pens. The catapults, even — every goblin flung over that wall was a thief delivered to where the food was. None of it was war, friend. Not one raid of it. It was *harvest*.

VANDAL: *(grave)* The hill giants of the North are gathering food. Grain, and cattle, and — I will say it plainly — whatever else that knife was made to dress. Gathering it, and hauling it, all of it, to a single mouth. Their queen.

VANDAL: *(quoted, cold, hushed)* Guh.

VANDAL: *(softer, chilling)* Queen Guh. A mountain of hunger somewhere out in the hills, with every hill giant in the North for her foraging party. Golden Fields was never the enemy, friend. Golden Fields was the pantry.

VANDAL: *(reflective)* The Ordning, friend, is broken — the old ordering of giantkind, that held storm above cloud, and cloud above fire and frost, and all of them above the hills. And when the ordering of the world goes, it is the hungriest who rise first. Mark that. No one in Faerûn hungers like Guh.

[MUSIC: minor swell — 4s]

## [CLOSING — 30s]

VANDAL: *(reflective, drawing to a close)* And so the chapter closes on the walls of Golden Fields — granaries still standing, the abbey bell silent at last, the town counting what the season has cost it. Ahead of the crew, a queen to find, somewhere out in the hills — and beneath her, the harder question. Why now? Why is a mountain of hunger being fattened, and for what table? And south of all of it, the long road to Waterdeep — where, though they did not yet know it, an old storyteller was waiting to cross their path.

VANDAL: *(warm)* Rest well tonight, heroes. The hills will keep till morning.

VANDAL: *(warm, signature)* I am Vandal Lovelace. This has been a Tale of the True Hand.
